Bottom line (read this if nothing else)

  • Pick: the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex as the place to read memory content (single neurons), and the lateral temporal cortex as the place where writing with today's electrodes has actually helped memory. "One region" is the wrong frame: a memory is an index in the hippocampus plus content spread over cortex. Triangulate on a loop, not a spot.
  • Why Ezzyat 2018 skipped the hippocampus: a 49-patient study (Jacobs 2016) found that stimulating the hippocampus or entorhinal region during learning made recall worse by about 8–9%. Lateral temporal cortex was the site where stimulation helped, and it has many clinical electrodes.
  • Writing a specific memory has only been done in mice (engram reactivation, holographic optogenetics). In humans, what works today is nudging the state (closed-loop stimulation at bad-encoding moments, sleep cueing), with effects of roughly 10–35%. Content-specific writing in humans has one small, mixed study (Roeder 2024).
  • Neuralink records cortex, not hippocampus. 21 participants were confirmed in Jan 2026. Its data is not public. The public equivalents that matter for you are on DANDI (human hippocampus/amygdala single neurons) and OpenNeuro (the RAM data you already use).
  • Your idea has names already: "neural co-processor" (Rajesh Rao, 2019) and "hippocampal prosthesis" (Berger/Hampson/Song). Use them in outreach.
  • Bluntly on H-Bridge: your scalp classifier is the noninvasive cousin of Ezzyat's decoder, but your confirmatory result (ΔAUC 0.005 over behaviour, about half matched by eye channels) says scalp EEG is far too weak to trigger stimulation. Ezzyat's in-brain decoder reached AUC 0.61. That gap is a finding to report, not a failure to hide.

Final pick

Read side: hippocampus + entorhinal cortex at single-neuron level. It is where memory content is sparse and nameable (concept cells), where public human single-neuron data exists, and where every serious memory-prosthesis effort points. now for analysis of public data.

Write side: lateral temporal cortex for state-based help (the only region with a positive closed-loop result in humans), plus amygdala and prefrontal (sleep) as runners-up. Writing into the hippocampus itself needs patterned, neuron-scale stimulation, not big pulses. ~5 yrs for patterned human trials, 20+ yrs for content.

Your Phase 1 consequence: analyse DANDI 000004 (59 subjects, hippocampus and amygdala, recognition memory) to ask your same question at neuron level. Its scale makes it the obvious next rung.

Why Ezzyat 2018 stimulated lateral temporal cortex, not hippocampus

  1. Hippocampal stimulation had just failed. Jacobs 2016 (49 patients, 50 Hz during learning): recall dropped about 9% with entorhinal and 8% with hippocampal stimulation; no patient improved. Ezzyat et al. write that open-loop stimulation of the hippocampus and MTL "is unlikely to reliably improve memory".
  2. Lateral temporal activity predicts memory. In fMRI and iEEG, activity there during learning predicts later recall, and the region holds word meaning.
  3. Prior stimulation work pointed there (their phrase: "a prime target for memory modulation"), and in the same group's data lateral temporal stimulation beat other regions (Kucewicz 2018).
  4. Practicality. Electrodes go where the epilepsy doctors need them; lateral temporal contacts are common, so they could be chosen in most of the 25 patients.
  5. My interpretation SPECULATIVE: a large electrical pulse in the hippocampus scrambles a sparse code it is trying to write; in cortex it may instead push the network into a better encoding state. This is consistent with Titiz 2017 (gentle micro-stimulation of entorhinal white matter helped) but not proven.

D3 · The Neuralink lens

Hardware (public facts)

  • N1 "Link": 1,024 electrodes on 64 threads (company figures, via this guide) ESTABLISHED as reported.
  • 2019 research system: up to 3,072 electrodes, 32 per thread, 256 amplifiers per chip, ~19 kHz, 10-bit; spike detection by simple threshold crossings, no spike sorting, done in software (Musk 2019, JMIR).
  • Whether N1 detects spikes on the chip, and its exact radio bandwidth: UNVERIFIED (not in the sources I could open).
  • Why that matters (my arithmetic SPECULATIVE): 1,024 ch × 19.3 kHz × 10 bit ≈ 200 Mbit/s raw. A low-power implant radio carries a tiny fraction of that, so the chip must cut data ~100–1000×. Neuralink ran a public challenge for >200× lossless compression (Wikipedia). Threshold crossings (spike yes/no per channel per ms) are the cheap answer; sorting spikes into neurons is the expensive one they skip.

Where it goes and why cortex

  • Threads go into motor cortex for movement intent (PRIME/CONVOY etc.); the exact target (hand knob) UNVERIFIED.
  • Why not hippocampus: (1) the medical need (paralysis) lives in motor cortex; (2) threads are a few mm long, the hippocampus sits ~4–5 cm deep, below cortex the robot can see; (3) deep insertion crosses more vessels and tissue, so more risk; (4) movement decoding works from surface layers.
  • The 2019 paper says deeper insertion is possible, but only cortical results were shown.

Trial status

  • 21 participants enrolled worldwide, reported by Neuralink in late Jan 2026 (Bloomberg via Yahoo; Technology.org) ESTABLISHED as company report.
  • "26th patient" by June 2026: from one X post relayed by a blog (basenor) UNVERIFIED.
  • "Next-gen implant with ~3× channels" (16 Oct 2026): fan site with AI-generated label (allthings-elon) UNVERIFIED.
  • Registered trials: PRIME (NCT06429735), CAN-PRIME (NCT06700304), UAE-PRIME (NCT06992596), GB-PRIME (NCT07127172), CONVOY (NCT06710626), VOICE speech (NCT07224256), as listed in the guide; I did not open ClinicalTrials.gov itself.
  • Blindsight: FDA breakthrough designation Sept 2024; Musk said in Jan 2026 it was ready for a first human (India TV). A completed human Blindsight implant: UNVERIFIED.

Reaching deep structures: what it would take

  • Today's clinical route: SEEG depth leads in epilepsy patients; Behnke-Fried microwires at the tip give single neurons in hippocampus/amygdala (all the DANDI data). now
  • Neuropixels in humans: cortex first (Paulk 2022, Chung 2022), then 45 mm probes reaching hippocampus and cingulate in 9 patients during surgery (Brown et al. 2026 preprint), and hundreds of hippocampal neurons under anaesthesia (Katlowitz et al. 2026, Nature). All acute (minutes), not chronic. EMERGING
  • Chronic deep, high-count recording (months, thousands of neurons in hippocampus) does not exist in humans. Needs flexible long probes, a safe trajectory through cortex, and a medical reason to be there (epilepsy, later perhaps dementia). ~5 yrs for small trials, 20+ yrs for routine.

If I were Neuralink's research-strategy team

  1. Beachhead = epilepsy. Patients already have hippocampal electrodes; a memory decoder there is a credible add-on. SPECULATIVE
  2. Data that would change minds: a hippocampal decoder that names which item is being encoded/recalled, holds up across days despite drift, and a stimulation that helps recall by a large margin in a pre-registered trial.
  3. Most interesting part: closed-loop sleep stimulation (Geva-Sagiv 2023). It acts while the user does nothing, the effect is on consolidation, and the trigger is a simple slow-wave detector.
  4. Channel budget question (a great project for you): how many hippocampal neurons do you need to decode item identity at a given accuracy? Compute it from DANDI data by subsampling neurons. That one curve tells an engineer how many channels to build.
