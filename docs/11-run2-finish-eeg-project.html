Gaps and deviations to disclose (scalp)

  1. Duplicate-sample skips. 5 sessions failed because two events shared a sample (MNE refused to epoch). Not a listed reason. Disclose as a deviation; 381 sessions were analysed in total (4 more had no EEG file, a listed reason).
  2. Eye control threshold. The rule only said what to do if EOG gain ≥ EEG gain. The observed EOG gain was about half (0.0025 of 0.0050), with EEG − EOG still positive (p = 0.0016). The registration gives no rule for "half", so say so and describe it plainly.
  3. No power analysis. Stated in the registration (sample set by compute). Repeat that in the paper; the equivalence rule is what makes the null informative.
  4. Selection is ordered, not random. Ascending IDs are roughly recruitment order. Low risk, but mention it.
  5. Prior contact. Behaviour files of all participants were read before registration (for selection and word recallability). Disclosed in the registration; repeat it.

Both analyses were pre-registered before any confirmatory EEG was analysed (scalp EEG: osf.io/9ghzn, 22 October 2026; intracranial: osf.io/xn4v9, 23 October 2026), with the analysis code frozen and its SHA-256 checksums attached. All analyses labelled exploratory were registered in a dated addendum after the confirmatory results were known and do not affect the registered verdicts.

D7 · Exploratory plan: E1, E2 and E5

I checked the real event files before choosing. In the pilot session file (sub-LTP063_ses-0), words come about every 4.0 s (3.8 to 4.2 s), word display duration is not recorded (duration = n/a), recall has vocal-onset events (REC_WORD, 114 in that session), and there is a recognition test with EEG: 160 RECOG_TARGET events, every one a studied word of that session, with old/new response and confidence. So E5 is possible.

E1 in one paragraph

For end-times 0.0 to 1.6 s after word onset (0.1 s steps) and one pre-word window, compute power from data before that moment only, z-score with training-session statistics only, refit the registered models per end-time. Report the earliest time at which EEG-only AUC, and separately ΔAUC, reliably exceeds chance, plus the same curve from eye channels. Expectation from the confirmatory result SPECULATIVE: EEG-only AUC rises above 0.5, ΔAUC stays near 0.

E2 in one paragraph

Pick k words per list to "re-present": at random, by the non-neural model, by the combined model, by EEG alone, or by an oracle. Assume a re-presented, otherwise-forgotten word becomes recalled with probability b (swept 0.1 to 0.5, no literature value assumed). The key number is combined minus non-neural: the extra words recalled that EEG is responsible for. A theory curve marks scalp, in-brain (0.570) and Ezzyat 2018 (0.61) AUCs.

E5 in one paragraph

Pattern = channels × 8 frequencies, 0.2 to 1.6 s after the word appears. For each studied word: correlation between its encoding and recognition patterns, minus its correlation with other same-list words' recognition patterns. Test whether that item-specific similarity is above 0, and whether it is larger for confident hits and for recalled words. Repeat on eye channels. Caveat: recalled words were also retrieved during free recall, before the recognition test.

How a finding becomes real

Only 23 of the 149 eligible ltpFR participants have had their EEG opened. If E5 (or E1) shows something, write a new pre-registration with a fixed test and a smallest effect of interest, freeze the code, and run it once on the next 20 IDs. That is a clean confirmatory test of an exploratory finding, with no new data collection.

OSF addendum text (post as a dated public file before running anything)

Exploratory addendum 2 to "EEG at Study Predicts Later Recall" (osf.io/9ghzn). Date: [posting date].

Status at the time of writing. The registered confirmatory analysis has been run (verdict: failure, present but negligible; mean ΔAUC 0.005). The intracranial addendum (osf.io/xn4v9) has been run (verdict: ambiguous). The analyses below were planned after these results were known. They are exploratory, run on the same 20 confirmatory participants, and cannot change either verdict. The frozen confirmatory code is not modified; new code is in a separate folder and will be frozen (SHA-256 list) after testing on the 3 pilot participants and before running on the 20.

E1, pseudo-online decoding. Power at the 8 registered frequencies computed from causal windows ending 0.0 to 1.6 s after word onset (0.1 s steps) and from a pre-stimulus window ending at word onset; z-scoring uses training sessions only. Registered models and cross-validation, refitted per end-time. Outcomes: EEG-only AUC and ΔAUC per end-time (hierarchical bootstrap 95% CI); earliest end-time after which the CI stays above chance; the same for the 4 EOG channels.

E2, simulated re-presentation policy. Using regenerated held-out predictions from the frozen code (required to reproduce the frozen AUCs to 4 decimals), choose k = 1, 2, 3 words per list by: random, non-neural, combined, EEG-only, oracle. Assumed benefit: a chosen word that was not recalled becomes recalled with probability b = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5. Outcome: expected extra words recalled per list; key contrast combined minus non-neural. Plus an analytic curve of extra recall against classifier AUC under a binormal model.

E5, item-level encoding–recognition similarity. Feature vectors (channels × 8 frequencies, 0.2–1.6 s) at study and at the recognition-test presentation of the same word (RECOG_TARGET events). Item-specific similarity = r(own encoding, own recognition) − mean r(own encoding, recognition of other words from the same list). Tests: mean item-specific similarity > 0 across participants (one-sample Wilcoxon); within-person difference by recognition confidence and by recall status; the same on EOG channels.

Not run: personal versus pooled decoding (already registered and reported as Secondary 1); spectral versus ERP features (deferred).

Any exploratory effect will be described as exploratory. A confirmatory test, if warranted, will be pre-registered separately and run on the next 20 eligible participants (ascending ID), whose EEG has not been opened.

Writing up the null

  • Lead with the verdict and the rule that produced it, then the numbers. Don't bury it.
  • Say what the null rules out: a gain of 0.01 AUC or more, on average, for this pipeline and this kind of person and task. It does not rule out other features, other people, or in-brain recordings (which came out ambiguous).
  • Translate it: E2 gives the size in words per list. "About X extra words per 100 studied" is clearer than "ΔAUC 0.005".
  • Report that a small, real, word-specific signal exists (own > previous word; 95% CI excludes 0) and that about half of it is matched by eye channels.
  • No spin words like "trend", "promising" or "nearly significant" for the confirmatory results.

Framing: "a first step toward closed-loop memory support"

  • Say "we measured how much information a noninvasive encoding-state decoder adds over behaviour, the quantity a closed-loop system depends on".
  • Say "scalp EEG, as analysed here, adds too little to justify triggering an intervention; intracranial data were inconclusive".
  • Don't say the method "reads memories", "predicts memory" without the baseline, or "could help Alzheimer's patients".
  • Don't present E1, E2 or E5 as confirmatory, even if they look strong.
